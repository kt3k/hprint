Theorem (and_comm').
  For all propositions p and q, if p ∧ q, then q ∧ p.

Proof.
Let p and q be propositions. Assume p ∧ q (call this h). We must show that
q ∧ p. We prove the two parts in turn.

  We must show that q. This holds by h.2.

  We must show that p. This holds by h.1.

∎

Theorem (or_elim).
  For all propositions p, q and r, if p ∨ q, p → r and q → r, then r.

Proof.
Let p, q and r be propositions. Assume p ∨ q (call this hpq), p → r (call
this hpr) and q → r (call this hqr). We must show that r. We distinguish
cases according to hpq.

Case inl.
  Assume p (call this hp). We must show that r. This holds by hpr hp.

Case inr.
  Assume q (call this hq). We must show that r. This holds by hqr hq.

∎

Theorem (contrapositive).
  For all propositions p and q, if p → q and ¬q, then ¬p.

Proof.
Let p and q be propositions. Assume p → q (call this h) and ¬q (call this
hq). We must show that ¬p. Assume p (call this hp). We must derive a
contradiction. This holds by hq (h hp).

∎
